4Pull out facts with proof

  • Own pages are trusted; ranges are ignored.
  • A Germany-wide total is never counted as Berlin.
For sales: a reason to call, and a quote to point to.
Cost of this step about $0.02 per company

Validation

How the data was checked

Three layers: text matching, strict data rules and AI checks. Every value traces back to the page it came from.

1 · Text matching

  • A fact's quote must be on the saved page (exact, or at least 80% of its words).
  • Every number, phone and e-mail must appear in that quote.
  • A LinkedIn person is kept only if the link and name were in the search results; a job-ad contact only if they are in the ad.
  • Facts are rechecked at every export; the dashboard shows the quote and source for every value.

2 · Data types and rules

  • Every fact must fit a strict schema: field, category, number, scope and date.
  • Values must be one of fixed categories (e.g. WEG, rental or own stock; meters read by hand or remote).
  • Rules: ranges ignored, approximate figures labelled, company-wide totals never counted as Berlin, old facts never read as current, Berlin required.
  • Duplicates merged; brokers, metering competitors and service firms set aside, with hand checks of samples.

3 · AI checks

  • The reading model must quote its source and pick a category for every fact.
  • When the code rejects a fact, the model gets the reason and one repair attempt.
  • A second AI pass looks for facts that are still missing.
  • In the contact search, the model keeps only people whose result shows a current job at the company.
  • A separate AI reviewer double-checked facts in the pilots; it was dropped for the full run for speed and cost, and the code checks stayed.
Design decisions

Built to be trusted and cheap to run

Five choices that shape the list.

Proof for every fact

Each fact links to the quote and page it came from.

Fit and reachability kept separate

Sales sees both why a firm matters and how to get through.

AI reads, rules score

The model only collects facts; plain rules rank them, so every score is explainable and cheap.

Company sources and exact numbers first

Facts come from the company's own pages first; an exact published number beats an approximate one, and approximate figures are labelled.

No logins, contacts stay private

Only public pages are read, and the contact list stays inside the sales team.

Limits and next steps

What's still hard, and what comes next

Honest gaps today, then the natural next steps to add value and to scale.

Limits today

  • Personal e-mails are rarely public.
  • LinkedIn results often lack job titles.
  • Metering provider and contract dates are rarely published (1–2% of companies).

Next steps: more value per lead

  • Confirm who manages the apartments. Add job titles from a licensed LinkedIn data provider, so every contact shows whether they run buildings.
  • Verified personal e-mails. Check likely addresses with an e-mail verification service before outreach.
  • Fresh buying signals. Re-check job ads and websites weekly and flag new hiring, growth or a provider change.
  • Learn from sales. Sync leads and call outcomes with the CRM, and tune the fit score on the leads that converted.

Next steps: scale

  • All of Germany. Run the map search city by city and postcode by postcode, and add the regional VDIV and housing-association directories.
  • Refresh only what changed. Keep the saved pages and re-read only pages that changed, so repeat runs cost a fraction.
  • Scheduled runs. A monthly run keeps the list current without manual work.
